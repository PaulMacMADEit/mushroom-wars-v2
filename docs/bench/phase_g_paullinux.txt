4. Diagnosis — why didn't SM% reach the 20% target?
================================================================

G1+G2 collapsed 11% of step time (host-side mask + pack) down to 0.2%.
That's a clean win on the work we targeted. But SM% only moved 2.8% →
4.6% (1.6×) — well short of the 20% target the plan set.

The Phase E doc identified the bottleneck as kernel-launch-bound: each
step_chunk completes faster than Python can queue the next launch. G1+G2
removed the host-side work that was *between* launches, but did not
change the per-step host-device sync that gates the launch queue itself:

  step_chunk → (sync on `result["dones"]`) → auto_reset → next iteration

Specifically, sim/envs/jax_vec_env.py:332 does
  done_np = np.asarray(done)
which forces a JAX-to-host transfer that blocks until step_chunk's
kernel completes. Auto-reset and per-env episode tracking depend on
the host-side dones, so the sync is structural, not incidental.

Per-step wall-clock breakdown from the unsync'd train run (320 samples
at 1s, 14 updates in 317.7s = ~22.7s/update → 64 rollout steps + learn):
  ~245 ms/step on rollout (matches phase-attr), of which:
    - step_chunk kernel launch + execution
    - device→host dones transfer (forced sync)
    - auto_reset (Python loop on dones)
    - launch overhead before the next step's encode/pack/act/step
  ~6 s/update on PPO learn (k_epochs * minibatches * forward+backward)

The 245 ms/step is the binding constraint. Of that, the kernel itself
runs in <30 ms (per Phase E's nvidia-smi dmon spot-check during a
single kernel) — the remaining 215 ms is launch-queue idle.

5. Decision
================================================================

G1+G2 are correct, fast (6-14% wall improvement), training is healthy.
Plan §1 criterion 3 (pack+mask <2%) is met. The SM% target is missed
because the binding constraint moved to the per-step dones-sync,
which is exactly the sub-phase the plan flagged as conditional:

  > §3.4: "If profile after G2 still shows kernel-launch idle time, G3
  >        explicitly batches the per-step `dones` pull (one combined
  >        transfer per rollout) instead of per-step. Don't pre-implement
  >        this — measure first."

Now we have measured. Two paths forward — pick before continuing:

**Option A: Stop here.** Ship G1+G2 as a clean win on host-side work.
Don't pursue further perf since:
  - Training is already fast (win-rate hits 0.98 in ~5 min on
    random_legal — the baseline benchmark is exhausted).
  - The next bottleneck (opponent strength, not throughput) is what
    b3 self-play sweep will probe; perf gains here don't help that.
  - Implementing async dones would require restructuring auto-reset
    and episode bookkeeping to defer past a sync barrier — non-trivial,
    ~half a day of careful work.

**Option B: Continue to G3a — async dones / batched done pull.** Lift
the per-step host sync. Expected gain: another 1.5-3× SM%, possibly
hitting 10-15% mean. Approach:
  - Keep dones on device through the K-step rollout; pull all T*K
    dones in one combined transfer at the end of the rollout.
  - Defer auto_reset until rollout completion; either do it once
    per rollout or pipeline with the PPO learn step.
  - Episode tracking (ep_return, ep_length, completed_episodes)
    needs to read dones in batched form too.

Recommended: **Option A** unless self-play work in b3+ exposes a
training-throughput bottleneck. The marginal SM% from G3a doesn't
translate to learning gains on the current benchmark.

(Phase G4 — opponent-inside-JIT for neural opponents — remains
as a separate gated follow-up; orthogonal to G3a.)
